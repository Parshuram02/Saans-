Empirical Validation

Leave-One-Year-Out Back-Test

To prevent overfitting and prove credibility, BreathAhead was rigorously back-tested across 22 test days spanning peak burning seasons (Oct 20 – Nov 20) in 2024 and 2025. For each test day, the target test year was completely excluded from the climatology index.

Method Precision@50 Recall@50 Mechanism
BreathAhead Combined (Ours) 37.7% 37.0% History prior + 48h spatial diffusion
Yesterday's Fires (Persistence) 24.6% 23.9% 24h raw fire persistence
History Only (Climatology) 41.3% 40.8% Multi-year ±7d seasonal window
Key Finding: Raw persistence ("Yesterday's fires") suffers severely (24.6% precision) because farmers shift burning fields once harvesting completes. BreathAhead combines historical agricultural cycles with recent neighborhood ignition diffusion, delivering far more reliable spatial coverage.

Architecture & Methodological Rigor

Transparent formulation with zero black-box obscurity

🛰️

1. NASA FIRMS VIIRS Ingestion

Thermal radiation detections from the SNPP / NOAA-20 VIIRS instruments (375-meter nadir resolution). Filtered strictly for nominal and high confidence detections across Punjab and Haryana state boundaries.

🧭

2. Upwind Vector Transport Model

Surface wind vectors from Open-Meteo determine upwind arrival cones (±30° tolerance). Direct Haversine distance divided by local wind velocity computes physical smoke arrival times (ETA).

⚖️

3. Dual Climatology + Diffusion Scoring

Grid-level risk blends multi-year historical seasonal priors (±7-day window) with 48-hour spatial neighborhood ignition diffusion to reliably predict next-day burning hotspots.

⚠️

Explicit Model Limitations & Transparency

  • Satellite Observation Gap: VIIRS provides 2–4 satellite overpasses daily. Cloud cover or orbital blindspots may temporarily hide active surface fires.
  • First-Order Wind Transport: Arrival ETA assumes uniform straight-line travel using city surface winds. Real smoke transport is governed by planetary boundary layer heights and atmospheric wind shear.
  • Threshold Calibration: Threat level cutoff values (50 and 200) are empirically chosen for operational triage and remain open to calibration against ground PM2.5 sensors.
